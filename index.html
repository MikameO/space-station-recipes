NanoTrasen ChemDB is an unofficial community tool for Space Station 14, an open-source multiplayer game. All content — including reagent names, chemical formulas, synthesis recipes, toxicity ratings, delivery methods, and antagonist strategies — describes fictional in-game mechanics. Real chemical names are used by the game for flavor; the properties, reactions, and effects shown here do not reflect real-world chemistry. This site provides no real-world medical, chemical, or safety guidance.

Ready recipes

The best mixture this casing can hold for each job, found by an exhaustive sweep rather than a guess. Material counts every unit of base chemistry the mixture costs. Steps counts the distinct reactions it needs, which is the part that eats a shift: ANFO is four, cyclonite six, octogen eight, and reagents sharing a chain share its steps. HE is what one indirect hit does to the castes that actually hold a line, T2 and up, two or three tiles away: destroyed, then crippled. A dash means the round cannot reach them at all, which is true of every grenade here. Resin walls are not on that list because no casing can break one by damage: a plain wall takes 900 and the strongest ordnance delivers 720. They fall to a sticky charge, which deletes them within a tile whatever it is filled with, so the breach row holds the cheapest filling that still goes off. Denial ranks burning ground per unit of material, four tiles or wider, because entering a flame costs a flat 45 heat whatever its intensity. Click a row to load it.

What it does to them

The roster the in-game demolitions simulator offers, showing what the mixture above leaves of each one. A xeno takes double damage from an explosion, less its explosion armour, so the figure on a card runs ahead of the epicentre damage above. Fire meets no armour at all and is counted over the window you set: nobody stands in a flame until it dies out, and the hive pats a burning xeno at ten stacks a second. Shrapnel is still left out, so the real thing is worse than shown.
